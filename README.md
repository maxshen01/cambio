# Cambio

Cambio is a four-player card game with an in-memory Node.js/Socket.IO server and a React/Vite client. Lobby setup and memorization lead into playable clockwise turns: deck draws allow direct discard or own-hand replacement, and public discard-pile draws require replacement. Both draw sources are available through the board. Active-game departures and unexpected handler faults return connected survivors to an undealt lobby for a fresh game.

This README is the single maintained account of implementation capabilities, limitations, and coverage. Read it alongside the relevant source, tests, and design specifications before planning or implementing work. Source and tests establish behavior if this summary becomes stale.

## Current implementation

- **Lobby:** validated, normalized, unique display names; four stable seats; server-confirmed membership; Ready/Unready before dealing, including readiness before the room fills. Four connected, ready players trigger the deal.
- **Cards and memorization:** two shuffled decks including jokers, with four cards per player. Each recipient receives only their own bottom two opening faces. A separate Ready confirmation hides those faces and removes their IDs from client display state. Rejections preserve the authorized view. Departures during memorization clear the deal and readiness while retaining survivor seats, allowing a replacement and fresh deal.
- **First active turn:** after broadcasting the fourth memorization `playerReady`, the server uniformly selects one connected player, sets `phase: "active"` and `turnStep: "awaitingDraw"`, and broadcasts exactly one `turnChanged`. One injectable random generator controls opening shuffling, starter selection, and refill; it defaults to `Math.random`. Duplicate confirmations cannot start another turn; readiness is unavailable after activation.
- **Deck turns:** only the current player can draw once from the deck. The pending card stays outside their hand and only its owner receives its face ID until a valid power use reveals it. Direct discard completes immediately for cards without powers; powered deck draws immediately offer Use/Don't use while own-hand replacement remains available; replacement puts the card facedown into an occupied own-hand slot and discards the displaced card without activating a power. Completed resolutions clear pending state, mark the discard top eligible, and advance once clockwise by stable seat order, wrapping seat four to seat one. Rejections preserve state and are requester-only.
- **Refill:** successful draws or discards retry refill when the deck has at most two cards and the discard has at least three. Older discards are shuffled into the deck bottom, preserving the remaining deck order and top two discard cards. Refill emits no extra public event or pile counts.
- **Discard turns:** only the current player awaiting a draw can click a visible discard top, sending `drawCard` with `source: "discard"`. All seated players receive the drawn face before the exposed older-top pile update (or `null` for an emptied pile). The pending card stays outside the hand; the exposed top is marked `exposed`, or its status is null when empty. Empty-source requests return `DISCARD_EMPTY`. Direct discard is disabled in the UI and returns `MUST_SWAP_DISCARD_DRAW` without mutation if requested through the protocol; own-hand replacement uses the ordinary swap flow and advances once. No powers activate.
- **Draw source and permissions:** every `cardDrawn` carries required `source: "deck" | "discard"` from the server alongside the recipient-authorized face view. Client state retains source with the pending card, displays public opponent faces, and strips face IDs from facedown views. Older-top updates, including `null`, preserve pending discard draws, request locks, and replacement eligibility. `canReplace` permits either source; `canDiscardDrawn` permits deck draws only. Swap, turn change, new deal/lobby reset, and connection loss clear the pending card and source; rejected actions preserve them and release request locks. Server and client must use the source-bearing protocol together.
- **Board:** hands rotate around the local seat, all opening faces are removed on activation, and the current player receives a border and accessible turn marker. Setup readiness marks disappear during play. Ready Up and Call Cambio remain visible and disabled. The single shared area below the piles shows the drawn card upright, associated with its drawer. Deck, discard-pile, shared-card discard, and own-slot replacement controls follow eligibility and disable immediately while pending. Deck and discard drawing share a request lock, including overlapping clicks before rendering. Empty discard areas reserve invisible, noninteractive space. Server events update the board; rejections preserve it and restore eligible controls. Brief draw/discard/swap movements do not delay state processing and respect reduced-motion preferences. Draw movements originate at the server-confirmed source pile, including the reserved discard position after a sole-card draw; an older-top update preserves the movement without restarting it.
- **Artwork:** public card images are preloaded, decoded, and cached; failed loads expose a retry action. Deck construction retains card identities and power metadata.
- **7-10 backend peeks:** a powered deck draw sends owner-only `powerAvailable` after `cardDrawn`, without changing the discard or starting resolution. Direct `usePower` with `{}` validates and starts resolution, broadcasts `powerResolutionStarted` then `powerCardRevealed`, and enters `awaitingTargets`. Direct `skipPower` discards and completes without a separate reveal. The retained `discardDrawnCard` entry path still starts an awaiting-choice power. `choosePowerTargets` validates the selection and sends recipient-specific `powerPeekResult`: only the user receives its face; others receive facedown slot references. The card stays held until Done, without a timer. Skip before/during selection or Done after a peek completes through ordinary cleanup/refill/clockwise progression, with a pile update before one turn change. Invalid and duplicate requests preserve state and send requester-only errors.
- **Client peek state/actions:** power notifications retain the shared held card and apply its public reveal without another draw movement. Owner-only prompts and temporary authorized peek targets remain separate from baseline facedown hands; other viewers retain only facedown target references. `usePower()`, `choosePowerTargets({ playerId, handSlotId })`, `skipPower()`, and `finishPowerPeek()` enforce stage/ownership eligibility with one immediate turn-action lock. Done hides peek targets through the view selector while retaining the private buffer for rejection recovery; successful completion, turn change, new deal, abort/lobby reset, and connection loss erase it. Ordinary draw/replacement/discard controls remain disabled during resolution. Actions check the latest reducer state before React renders; stale notifications and detached socket callbacks cannot restore power state.
- **Peek UI:** immediately after a powered deck draw, the owner sees the highlighted held card, explanation, and Use/Don't use. Clicking Use or the held card activates; clicking an own-hand slot before activation still replaces the drawn card. The server-confirmed public reveal enables legal targets. A legal single-target click submits immediately, with no Confirm selection button. Two-target powers keep the first selection local and allow deselection; the final valid click submits. Selection and controls lock while pending, and only authorized server results reveal faces. Rejected target selections clear for retry. Done hides peek faces immediately; rejection restores the prompt and authorized faces. Other players see the public held card and facedown peek outlines without power controls or additional messages. Keyboard activation and focus on legal targets/Done remain supported. Peek artwork loads independently of the held card; image failure leaves Done available. Fake-socket components and connected four-board jsdom flows cover the shortened interactions, privacy, cleanup, and Skip stages. Browser and visual acceptance remain unverified.
- **Connection and setup recovery:** initial connection failure can retry on the same socket while preserving the entered name. Established connection loss clears membership, pending requests, and private display state and requires reload; late events cannot restore the lost session. Unexpected lobby/setup handler faults are logged, reset the shared room while preserving its object identity, and disconnect all clients, including observers. The server remains listening. Expected domain errors remain requester-only and do not reset the room.
- **Active-game recovery:** a seated departure marks that player disconnected in `playerDisconnected`, then sends `gameAborted` with `PLAYER_DISCONNECTED`, their ID, and the reset lobby. Unexpected active-handler faults, including after partial mutation, log and send `gameAborted` with `SERVER_ERROR` and no player ID. Connected survivors retain membership and seats; all round state and readiness clear in the same shared state object. Reentrant cleanup is suppressed. Clients clear pending requests, private cards, piles, turn state, and movements on abort; ordinary readiness and replacement-seat joins allow a fresh deal and renewed play without survivor reloads. Recovery also recognizes the reserved `finalRound` phase; final-round gameplay remains unimplemented.

- **Jack blind swap:** drawing a Jack from the deck immediately offers Use/Don't use while replacement remains available. Use publicly reveals the held Jack before selection. The owner selects two occupied slots belonging to different players (own/opponent or opponent/opponent). The first card can be deselected; the second valid click immediately submits the swap. The server validates both positions before exchanging their cards, then sends a face-free `cardSwapped` power result, the Jack discard, and one clockwise turn change. Cards stay facedown; stable slots and hand sizes are preserved. The client retains the held Jack until the pile update, supports keyboard selection and immediate request locks, clears rejected selections for retry, and preserves hand exchange movements through discard/turn events with reduced-motion support. Skip works before or after activation; Jack has no peek or Done stage. Discard draws, replacements, and displaced Jacks grant no power.

- **Black queen/king peek then swap:** black queens privately reveal one occupied slot; black kings reveal two distinct slots, including two in the same hand. Targets may belong to anyone. Done hides the faces locally and enters a fresh swap-or-skip selection with no cards preselected, retaining the public held card and current turn. No Done success event is emitted; the client opens the next stage immediately, preserves hidden faces for rejection recovery, and orders subsequent requests on the same socket. The later swap can use any occupied pair belonging to different players, whether peeked or not; the second valid click submits immediately, reusing Jack's hidden exchange and animations. Skip works before activation, during peek selection, and after Done; viewing faces requires Done first. A successful swap or Skip automatically discards and advances once. Both stages retain ordinary-action blocking; resolution, abort, new deal, turn change, and local loss clear retained peek data.

The activation protocol requires matching server and client versions: `usePower` now sends `{}` and target submission uses `choosePowerTargets`; the previous combined request is unsupported.

## Limitations and temporary behavior

- All card powers are implemented for active turns. Matching and final-round interactions remain deferred. Only deck draws committed to power resolution activate powers; discard draws, replacements, and displaced cards never activate them.
- Matching, gifts, penalties, hand-limit additions, Call Cambio, final rounds, scoring, and results are not implemented. Reserved server-state fields do not establish working gameplay. Only implemented protocol branches are exposed in shared public types. Play has no normal round-ending flow yet.
- Automatic reconnection/session resume is unsupported. State is not persisted. `gameState` is reserved and is not emitted.

## Running and validating

For manual playtesting, the server's `npm.cmd run dev` command supports `PLAYTEST_TOP_CARDS` in its local `.env`: an ordered list of ranks or exact faces for the first deck draws after dealing. It preserves all physical cards and reapplies on fresh deals. Normal startup ignores the setting; production playtesting is blocked. See [development deck ordering](code/Development.md#development-deck-ordering) for syntax and usage. This helper has no automated unit/integration coverage.

Use [Development](code/Development.md) for installation, environment setup, [local startup](code/Development.md#running-locally), [focused checks](code/Development.md#commands-and-checks), diagnostics, and the [pre-merge gate](code/Development.md#pre-merge-gate). See the [server](code/server/README.md) and [client](code/client/README.md) entry guides for package-specific links.

The current user-performed manual checkpoint takes approximately four minutes:

1. Restart the server, reload and join four tabs, ready all players to deal, then confirm memorization separately. Confirm the initially empty discard area cannot be clicked.
2. Have the starter draw from the deck; only the drawer sees its face. For a non-power card, click it to discard. For a power card, Use/Don't use appears immediately: click the held card or Use, then a legal target (own hand for 7/8, opponent for 9/10), then Done. Alternatively skip immediately or replace an own-hand card before activation. On the next turn, click the sole discard top.
3. Confirm every tab sees the held face in the shared area, the discard pile becomes invisible, and movement originates at the discard position. Direct discard is disabled; only the drawer can replace an own-hand card.
4. Replace an occupied own-hand card. Confirm facedown replacement, the displaced discard top, an empty shared area, and exactly one clockwise advancement.
5. Have the next player draw from the deck and discard, creating two discards. On the following turn, draw from discard; confirm the older top appears while the public held card remains available for replacement. Replace it and check the same completion behavior.
6. Confirm other players cannot draw or resolve the held card. On a later deck turn, replace an own-hand card to check that deck resolution still works. All power effects are active; deterministic power coverage is automated.

For manual Jack acceptance, draw a Jack and compare four tabs: only the owner sees Use/Don't use immediately, and own-hand replacement remains available before activation. Click the held card or Use; the public reveal must precede target selection. Select one card, deselect it, and verify same-player second targets are disabled. The second valid card click must exchange the two backs, discard the Jack, clear outlines, and advance once, without confirmation. Check both own/opponent and opponent/opponent pairs, Skip before/after activation, keyboard selection, and reduced-motion behavior. Visual acceptance remains unverified.

For manual peek acceptance, draw a 7-10 and compare four tabs. The owner alone sees the immediate explanation and Use/Don't use. Click the held card or Use and verify public reveal precedes legal target selection. A target click submits immediately; its face appears only for the owner after the server result. Done hides it immediately before discard and advancement; there is no timer. For a black queen/king, select one/two peek targets, click Done, and verify no swap cards are preselected. Choose any fresh pair from different players: the second click submits immediately. Repeat with Skip before activation, during selection, and after Done; verify keyboard focus and readable control spacing. Image failures and rejected requests use automated fixtures.

The active-game recovery checkpoint takes approximately five additional minutes:

1. Begin play with four tabs using the setup above. Draw a card, then close another player's tab.
2. Confirm survivors return to an unready lobby with empty card areas and their original seats. Join a replacement in the vacant seat, ready all four to deal, and confirm memorization separately.
3. Complete another ordinary turn; confirm the fresh deal is playable without survivor reloads.
4. Separately stop the backend while connected. Confirm affected tabs clear private state and require Reload to rejoin. Restart the backend afterward; unexpected handler faults are covered by automated injection checks.

Browser and visual acceptance remain user-performed; non-browser checks do not establish them. Individual check results and manual validation reports belong in chat or authorized GitHub updates.
